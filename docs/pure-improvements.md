# Pure improvements

A pure improvement here is a correctness, strictness, or completeness fix that does not give up latency, throughput, availability, or a Raft safety property. Batching, pipelining, leases, PreVote, and CheckQuorum stay as in Prytolyn.

## Considered and rejected (tradeoff)

- Do not send AppendEntries until the leader has fsynced. That would serialize disk and network and add commit latency. Paper 10.2.1 allows the parallel send. The quorum rule above is the safety fix.
- Do not count any peer until every peer has fsynced, including delaying the follower ack until a later barrier beyond the existing force-before-ack. The existing group commit already acks after fsync. An extra barrier would add latency.
- Report leadership-transfer success at `TimeoutNow` send time. That is fewer messages to wait for and a weaker completion signal.
- Drive leases from `DateTime.UtcNow`. Wall time can jump. The monotonic clock is the non-tradeoff.
- Raise `PreVoteLeaderLeaseFactor` or the election timeout to hide stalls. That spends availability.
- Shorten the read lease to `min(election timeout, 2 heartbeats)` so PreVote can stay gated at two heartbeats. That made the steady-state lease 80 ms on a host whose election timeout is 150 ms. The lease is the election timeout, measured from send time, and PreVote and RequestVote use that same window. See the vote-gate note below.
- Refuse client proposals while leadership transfer is still catching the target up. The thesis refuses new proposals only after `TimeoutNow`. Refusing earlier drops availability during catch-up and does not change the safety argument.
- Block the Raft thread until an asynchronous apply finishes before taking a snapshot or restoring one. That stalls heartbeats and elections for the duration of apply. The same ordering is done by deferring the snapshot until `FinishApply`, which adds nothing while apply is idle.
- Lengthen the socket fixture's election timeouts, or retry `Put` when leadership moves, to hide a stalled writer. A refused, reset, or timed-out connect retries the same frame, and a discarded write is sent again. Raising the fixture spends availability to mask a transport stall. `ConnectFailuresAndReset_originalPutStillCommits` uses the same 120/350/600 ms timeouts as `ThreeNodeTcp_electsExactlyOneLeader_andPutReplicates`.
- Cap every socket write at 75 ms. A 512 KiB AppendEntries or a snapshot chunk on a slow link is torn and then retried forever. The 75 ms budget applies to connect only.
- Count AppendEntries acks toward the read lease while a leadership transfer is pending. The target can win while the old leader's lease is still valid. Those acks are ignored. CheckQuorum still counts, so the leader does not step down for lack of contact.
- Drop an apply whose store epoch changed during decode. The command is then missing from the state machine. The apply retries the same mutation, and skips it when the index is already covered by a restore.
- Evict the oldest unsatisfied ReadIndex once 64 are waiting. The evicted client never observes that round. Pending reads stay until a round confirms them or the leader steps down.
- Leave an idle ReadIndex waiting for the heartbeat timer. That adds up to one heartbeat of latency. If no round is in flight, one round starts immediately.

## Lease during transfer and the advertised bound

After `TimeoutNow` the old leader stays leader and keeps heartbeating, and it does not count those acks toward the quorum lease or the linearizable lease. The window stays open until the transfer is observed or aborted. An ack for an AppendEntries sent inside the window does not re-arm the lease after the abort. `Phase123ReviewTests.Item2_heartbeatDuringTransfer_doesNotRearmTheLease`.

Every AppendEntries carries the leader's lease bound, which is the leader's election timeout. A follower that has seen a bound gates PreVote and RequestVote on that full bound since its last contact. A follower that has not seen a bound uses its own election timeout. Uniform configurations are unchanged, because the bound equals that timeout. `Phase123ReviewTests.MixedTimeout_fastVoter_doesNotVoteInsideTheLeadersLease`, `MixedTimeout_slowVoter_gateFollowsTheLeadersLease`.

Thesis §6.4.1 assumes a bound on clock drift. `SetClockDriftBound` sets ρ, default 0. The leader's lease is `bound × (1 − ρ)`, measured from AppendEntries send time. Followers keep gating on the full advertised bound, so a follower clock that runs faster cannot vote while the leader's lease is still valid. `Phase3SafetyRepairTests.SkewedFollowerClock_leaderLeaseShrinksByDriftBound`.

## Thesis-baseline behaviours and their measured cost vs 9ae58dd

Thesis section 4.2.3: a server disregards RequestVote received within the minimum election timeout of hearing from a current leader, unless the request carries the leadership-transfer flag. This code uses that window for PreVote and for RequestVote. That gate is the thesis, so it is not a row in the table above and it is not a tradeoff against the thesis.

The read lease is the leader's election timeout measured from AppendEntries send time. On the host that timeout is 150 ms, not two heartbeats (80 ms). `TimeoutNow` clears the lease and bypasses the vote gate.

Measured on the fake clock, against 9ae58dd:

- Uniform timeouts, one heartbeat of stagger. T = 150 ms, heartbeat = 40 ms, the second partition is 40 ms after the first. A new leader exists exactly 150 ms after the second partition, and the old leader's lease is already false (`Phase123ReviewTests.Item2_staggeredContact_failoverDoesNotOverlapTheLease`, `Phase2ReadElectionTests.Finding3_staleLeaderLease_expiresWhenPreVoteWindowOpens`). On 9ae58dd RequestVote was not gated, so the earlier node could win about one heartbeat sooner. The 150 ms is the election timeout the thesis already requires. Nothing past that gate showed up in these two runs.

- Mixed timeouts (`ElectionUnavailabilityTests.IsolateLeader_successorCommitsNewPut_windowWithinPaperBound`). The leader's timeout is 80 ms, the candidate's is 200 ms, and the voter it needs is configured at 400 ms. The leader advertises 80 ms. The successor commits at exactly 200 ms, which is the candidate's own election timer. On 9ae58dd the window was about 200 to 220 ms. Before the bound was advertised, this voter waited its own 400 ms. That extra wait is gone.

- Restart inside a lease. A node whose persisted term is already greater than 0 treats its start as contact with a leader and refuses PreVote and RequestVote for its own election timeout W. That is required for lease safety: a restart inside the old leader's lease must not grant a vote that lets a new leader commit while the old leader still serves the lease (`Phase123ReviewTests.RestartedNode_insideTheLease_doesNotGrantAVote`, `Phase3SafetyRepairTests.RestartedNode_insideTheLease_doesNotGrantAVote`). A brand-new node at term 0 may vote immediately (`TermZeroNode_grantsAVoteImmediately`). The wait is not added on top of the election timer. A candidate's own timer is already at least W, and a voter will not grant before its own W. On the fake clock, `DurableRestartTests.RestartAllThree_logsKeepCommitted_newTermProposeReappliesKv` elects at 400 ms. The leader's timeout is 80 ms and the two voters' timeouts are 400 ms, so 400 ms is the voters' own gate, not an extra delay past it. A uniform cluster pays nothing beyond the election timeout the thesis already requires.
